core / node_throughput.py

Learns each node's prefill/decode speed from llama.cpp timings, and sizes off-main work to what that node can actually finish inside its budget.

The defect it exists to kill

deep_research shipped every fetched page to the worker as a flat 40,000-char distill prompt with max_tokens=2048, on a flat 45s budget. On Nova (Gemma 4 E4B, -np 4, 32k/slot) that is ~12,500 prompt tokens against a ~300 tok/s prefill — 41 seconds of prefill before the first output token. Measured live on 2026-08-25 (req 08766aa1):

ConditionWall timevs the 45s budget
Solo, cold prompt, node completely idle42.7s (41.1s of it prefill)passes only if the model writes ~40 tokens
3 concurrent (the live Semaphore(3) shape)135s / 258s / 258sall fail

It was never slot exhaustion. /slots read 3 of 4 busy throughout — a slot was free and nothing queued. The requests were accepted and served; they were simply larger than the budget could pay for. Each one then timed out, was counted as a node fault against the circuit breaker, and silently degraded its source to raw truncated HTML — which fact_check consumes as if it were distilled evidence. The log window at the time held 1 success against 7 degradations.

The guard that was supposed to prevent this read the wrong number. It derived the char limit from max_context, which registry.py fills with args.max_context — the main 35B's 240,000-token window, not the worker's. So it pinned to its own 40,000-char ceiling on every call and never once constrained anything, while its comment claimed it was sizing "to the worker's context window".

The rule

Never send a request the node cannot finish in the time available. Both knobs — how much text we send (prefill seconds) and how much the model may write (decode seconds) — come from throughput measured on that node. When the budget cannot buy a useful answer, the honest move is to decline the call, not to post one that is arithmetically doomed.

How the rates are learned

llama.cpp returns a timings block on every response. NodeThroughput.observe() folds prompt_n/prompt_ms and predicted_n/predicted_ms into a per-node EWMA (α = 0.3), so the sizing re-tunes itself when the hardware, the model, or the concurrency changes.

A small sample is not a slow node. llama.cpp reports prompt_per_second over the whole prefill including fixed per-request overhead, so a tiny prompt measures absurdly low. On the same Nova that prefills at 300 tok/s, the 45s keepalive ping (max_tokens=1, content "ok") reports 13–25 tok/s, and a prompt-cache hit reports 4 tokens in 6.2s (~1 tok/s) because the count is the cache miss while the clock is the whole wait. Learning from either would collapse the estimate and starve every later distill. Hence the floors: MIN_PREFILL_SAMPLE_TOKENS = 512, MIN_DECODE_SAMPLE_TOKENS = 16.

How a request is sized

plan(budget_s, node_url) pays for the smallest request worth making, then spends the surplus:

  1. Take SAFETY_MARGIN_S (3s) off the budget — HTTP and the JSON encode/decode of a multi-KB body sit outside the two measured rates. (The permit queue is handled separately, by the caller's explicit _QUEUE_ALLOWANCE_S.)
  2. Cost the floors: MIN_CHARS (6,000) of prefill plus MIN_TOKENS (128) of decode. If that alone exceeds the budget, refuse. The floors are judged against the whole budget and the surplus against BUDGET_UTILISATION (85%) of it — folding the two together let the headroom veto the floor, so an unmeasured node refused everything and then learned nothing from the calls it never made.
  3. Split the surplus SURPLUS_TO_PREFILL (60%) toward reading, 40% toward writing — for fact extraction, reading more of the page beats writing a longer answer.
  4. Clamp to MAX_CHARS (40,000) and MAX_TOKENS (768), and to the node's advertised per-slot n_ctx when it has published one.

Chars-per-token converts the token budget into a char limit. CHARS_PER_TOKEN = 3.2 is only the prior: it is the mean density of the pages in the original incident (40,000 chars = 12,500 tokens is exactly 3.20), so it carries no margin at all, and deep_research fetches arbitrary URLs — markup/JSON-dense pages run ~2.6, Greek/Cyrillic ~1.8, CJK and minified assets ~1.3, where a prompt sized on 3.2 is 2.5× the tokens the budget paid for. Density is a property of the corpus, not of a node, so it is learned once and shared: every distill response reports prompt_n and the caller knows how many chars it sent. Measured live on real pages: 2.42. A safety factor biases toward denser text, which sends fewer chars — the safe direction.

The priors sit below the measured figures, because an optimistic prior blows the very first budget on an unmeasured node. But not arbitrarily below: a prior that cannot afford the floors declines every distillation, and a declined plan posts no request, so the node never produces the sample that would correct it. The two failure directions are not symmetric — an over-optimistic prior costs one call, which falls back to raw text and teaches the real rate; an over-pessimistic one latches. They are also solo-equivalent: plan() divides by the fan-out explicitly, so a loaded-looking prior would be divided a second time.

Concurrency is half of every rate

A rate without its concurrency is not a measurement. Measured on Nova going from one request to three concurrent:

alone3 concurrent (each)per requestaggregate
prefill304 tok/s220 tok/s×0.72×2.17
decode31.6 tok/s12.0 tok/s×0.38×1.14

The two halves scale differently for a physical reason: llama.cpp batches prefill across slots, so aggregate prefill throughput rises and each request degrades only mildly; decode is bandwidth-bound at a roughly fixed aggregate rate, so N concurrent decodes each get about 1/N. Hence prefill ~ solo/√N and decode ~ solo/N — which at N=3 predicts 176/10.5 where the node really did 220/12, i.e. conservative on both halves.

The caller must DECLARE its fan-out. A research wave plans every URL before any of them is in flight, so the node looks idle to all of them and each is sized as if it owned the box. This is the one thing the slow-rate tracker could not fix. Live evidence (req b86cdd59): four plans of ~19,936 chars / 596 tok were built at ~306/32 tok/s and then ran at 115/11 — 126s of work against a 45s budget. plan_distill takes the larger of the declared fan-out and "what is already running, plus me".

Samples are normalised the other way, back to a solo equivalent, so a rate measured under load is not penalised twice. Two subtleties that cost real debugging:

  • Peak, not acquire-time. Three requests that start together see 1, 2 and 3 in flight; recording the value at acquire made the first one claim it had run alone, so its loaded rate was learned as the node's solo speed.
  • Read inside the permit. _on_node_success runs after the gate's block, by which point its finally has discarded the bookkeeping — measured 3 inside the permit and 1 after it. For a while this made the whole normalisation dead code: loaded rates were stored as solo rates and divided a second time at plan time, and the distiller read ~4,600 chars of a page instead of ~15,000, less than the raw-text fallback it exists to beat. It looked healthy from outside, because small plans always finish.
  • No permanent rate floor. A "never plan below half the best rate ever seen" clamp was added while that poisoned estimator was learning ~4 tok/s on a node that does 31.6. A floor derived from a corrupted estimator is corrupted the same way — measured, it changed nothing — and it could not decay, so a genuinely downgraded node was pinned at a speed it could no longer reach, with timeouts returning no timings to ever move it. Removed. Fix the measurement, not the symptom.

Refusal is load-bearing

feasible=False means do not post. In req 08766aa1 the 4th URL reached the distiller with ~6s of deadline left, for a job needing ~27s, and posted it anyway — burning a worker slot on a request that could not finish and producing a ReadTimeout indistinguishable in the log from the three genuine 45s timeouts ahead of it. Callers now take their raw-text fallback instead.

A heartbeat is not evidence of health

The circuit breaker could not see any of this. The 45s keepalive is max_tokens=1 with the content "ok"; it completes in well under a second on a fully saturated node, so it cannot observe a 12k-token prefill overrunning its budget. In the live trace the breaker opened on three real timeouts at +101s and a keepalive closed it again at +108s — 7 seconds into a 60-second cooldown, while every real request was still failing.

_on_node_success therefore ignores keepalive and warmup labels when resetting the breaker. The asymmetry is deliberate: a failing heartbeat is still admissible, because it is the cheapest request we can make and if even that fails the node is genuinely unreachable. Evidence of sickness, never evidence of health.

Tests

tests/test_node_throughput.py (the estimator and its arithmetic) and tests/test_distill_budget_sizing.py (the research integration, the per-URL clock, and the breaker asymmetry). Both were mutation-checked: reverting max_tokens to 2048, ignoring the plan's char limit, posting a declined plan, moving the clock back to gather time, dropping the sampling floors, and letting a keepalive close the breaker are each killed by a test.
