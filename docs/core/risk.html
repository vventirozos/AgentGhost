core / risk.py — the throttle that finally consumes the risk estimate

Depth predicts failure. The calibrated composite predicts failure. Neither had a consumer. This module is the consumer — live (a steer) and offline (triage ranking).

The gap

Two failure predictors were measured on this agent and then used for nothing:

SignalMeasuredConsumer before this
Turn DEPTH17.8% failure at step 1 → 35.6% at 4 → 42.3% at 6 → 52.0% at 8 → 60.6% at 12 (§4H, 342 labelled trajectories)none
Calibrated compositeAUC 0.727 post-hocnone — _METACOG_ARBITER_ENABLED = False; logged, never acted on

So the agent could tell you it was probably failing, and did nothing differently.

Live half — the risk governor

score = 0.5·depth_prior + 0.3·effort_struggle + 0.2·failure_pressure
steer  ⟺  step ≥ 6  AND  score ≥ 0.50

Worked examples (verified against the implementation):

Turnscoresteer?
step 6, 5 clean calls0.30no — a normal working turn
step 8, 8 calls, 1 failure0.44no
step 10, 10 calls, 2-repeat, 1 failure0.51yes (measured ~56% failure)
step 12, 12 calls, 3-repeat, 2 failures0.63yes

The steer is one appended user-role message, fired at most once per request, telling the model to (1) state what is CONFIRMED vs ASSUMED, (2) run the single smallest distinguishing check, (3) stop and report honestly if it cannot name one. Option 3 is the point — at step 8 the agent is at ~52% and its best move is often to raise its hand, which it otherwise never does.

It yields to the futility breaker: that guard fires on the same pathology but NAMES THE FILE, so it is strictly better advice when it applies. The deference is one-way on purpose — a futility trip after a generic steer still carries new information and is allowed to fire. It is also withheld once the turn is already forced to finalize, because a turn told to write its final answer has its tool calls dropped downstream, and "run one small check" would then instruct an action the loop discards.

Backtest on the real corpus (1323 user turns, 2026-08-05)

Before trusting a trigger, check it fires at all — and on the right turns. Replaying the gate over every recorded user turn:

MeasureValue
turns the steer would fire on101 / 1323 = 7.6% — the tail, not everything
first-fire step6 (29), 7 (14), 8 (26), 9 (12), 10 (10), 11 (9), 12 (1)
failure rate, all resolved turns0.277
failure rate, fired-on turns0.543

So the gate selects a population with roughly double the base failure rate. That is a targeting result, not an effect: it says the steer is aimed at the right turns, not that it improves them. The experiment answers the second question.

Approximation: the backtest slices the end-of-turn tool list to each step index rather than replaying the live accumulation, so it is an upper bound on how EARLY the steer fires. The fire RATE is unaffected.

What the numbers claim

depth_failure_prior is empirical — those five points are measured on this agent's own corpus, linearly interpolated between them and held flat outside (extrapolating past step 12 would fabricate a number the corpus never supported). The blend is not: it is a documented heuristic ordering over three correlated signals (a deep turn has, by construction, made many tool calls), deliberately not called a probability. Its reachable range is [0.089, 0.803], not [0, 1].

A weight is not an importance. Measured decision spans: depth (w=0.5) contributes 0.214, effort (w=0.3) contributes 0.300, failures (w=0.2) contribute 0.200 — so the only calibrated term has the SMALLEST span and mostly acts as a constant offset. Depth alone cannot cross the threshold at any step (a 30-step turn that ran no tools scores 0.303). That is deliberate — a long turn making steady progress is not in trouble — but it means the gate is driven by the two uncalibrated terms, which is exactly why the steer text quotes the measured prior and never the blend.

The steer text also refuses two temptations: past step 12 it says "at 12 steps or more" rather than laundering a held-flat value as a measurement at the current depth, and it says "one common pattern" rather than "the cause is almost always" — the 342-trajectory result is an observational conditional rate, and no causal decomposition supports the stronger claim.

Whether it helps is not asserted here

The steer ships as the treatment arm of the risk_steer experiment. The answer comes from randomized live traffic, not from this page. introspect action='experiments' reads it.

Offline half — triage ranking (§4H item 2)

Reflection used to take the first max_failures failed trajectories in corpus order — the OLDEST ones — treating every failure as equally worth a scarce LLM critique. It now collects a bounded pool (6× the quota) and reflects its worst members.

  • One scale, always. The obvious implementation — calibrated score where it exists, shape proxy elsewhere — sorts one list on two incommensurable quantities, and measured worse than either input alone: Kendall τ 0.605 mixed vs 0.766 shape-only and 0.763 calibrated-only. On the live corpus 7 of the 8 joinable failures scored LOWER under the calibrated path than under their own shape proxy, so having a calibration sample demoted a trajectory and a real 12-step disaster fell out of the queue. The calibrated scale is now used only when it covers the ENTIRE pool (~5% join rate today, so shape in practice); it arms itself if coverage ever completes.
  • The stored composite is the RAW pre-Platt score, not a calibrated probability — a monotone transform of one, enough to rank within itself and not enough to compare against anything else.
  • Ranking only reorders trajectories that were already reflectable — it can never widen the candidate set, and never exceeds max_failures critiques.
  • The sort is stable, so an all-equal corpus degrades to exactly the old chronological behaviour.
  • seen_failures in the run report now counts the scanned pool ("reflected 3/18"), which is the accurate reading. With ranking off it is byte-for-byte the old early-break.
