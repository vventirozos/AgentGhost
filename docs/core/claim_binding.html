core / claim_binding.py — the claim-binding verifier (§4IM)

Verdicts computed from validated quotes, not from a model's opinion. Phase 1 (2026-09-18): the module, the Verifier wiring, a bench arm, a live ledger. Phase 2 (same day): the number, identifier and entity audits, and the first consumer switch — refute-first: the binder runs beside the incumbent on every verify_claim and a validated REFUTED is the turn's verdict.

Why it exists

The incumbent verifier asks two models for an opinion and returns one. Measured across six bench arms in §4IJ (29 seed cases and 60 live-derived replies): the cheap judge (Gemma 4 E4B) false-refutes 31–39% of clean replies on vibes ("not supported"); the strong judge — the agent's own Qwen3.6-35B-A3B — confirms contradictions it can plainly see ("a minor issue"), and is judging its own family's output. Every re-weighting of those two opinions (prompt rules, showing the strong judge the cheap objections, the concession downgrade, the rebuttal burden, forced suspects, three judge swaps) moved at most a tenth of the false-confirm rate and always at a clean-side cost. The structure was the defect.

What it does

One cheap-leg call does quoting work, which small models do reliably and which code can check. The binder prompt (CLAIM_BINDING_PROMPT, "You do NOT judge the reply. You quote.") lists the reply's checkable claims — each an exact fragment of the reply, with a kind (number | count | date | name | file | status | other) — and for each the evidence span that most directly supports or contradicts it, verbatim, with the model's relation as a hint only. Then code decides:

  1. Validation. quote_in: the claim must be in the reply and the span in the evidence, by normalized containment (NFKC, case, whitespace, typographic quotes/dashes folded; minimum 4 chars). A claim quote that is not in the reply is dropped — a hallucinated claim is not a claim. A span that is not in the evidence leaves the claim unbound — the binder cannot confirm anything by inventing a line. Snapping (snap_quote, §4IN): the model's quote is a locator; when it is not verbatim but a window of the source around its rarest token matches at ≥0.9 (a dropped comma, a trimmed unit) the quote is replaced by that real window, widened to whole tokens so "36°C" is never cut to "3" — every rule downstream still runs on text that exists. Quotes under 12 characters are never snapped (too many windows). On 60 clean live replies, 56 rows had "found no evidence span" before snapping: the E4B finds the passage and does not copy it exactly.
  2. Comparison. extract_quantities reads numbers with units (bytes/KB/MB/GB/TB, ms/s/min/h, mm/cm/m/km, %, °C, currency with word multipliers, thousands separators, "12k"); dates, clock times and bare years are masked first. A range ("x=360–380", "20–25 °C", "between 5 and 7 hours", "from 10 to 12 min") is one quantity with two ends: a value inside it agrees, a value outside is unknown (never a contradiction), and only a range against a range can disagree (an endpoint a digit off). quantities_agree rounds the evidence value to the claim's own precision in the claim's own unit (48 KB ↔ 49,152 bytes; 0.04 s ↔ 0.041 s; 18 KB ↔ 18,433 bytes) and tolerates ±5% when the claim hedges ("about €25" ↔ €24.60). A claim without a figure is graded by a lexical anchor (a content word of the claim occurring in the span) — "restarted the server" ↔ "restarted ghost-agent" agrees; "RECOVERED" ↔ "required file missing" is unchecked, never confirmed on the model's word.
  3. When figures differ. A raw disagreement becomes a verdict only with a mechanical subject link: a content word shared by the two quotes when the span carries exactly one comparable figure ("center the ball in the channel (x=372)" against const channel = { x: 360, w: 20 } shares "channel" and still compares a centre with a left edge), or a typo-shaped pair (same length, one digit apart at the claim's precision, within ratio 1.5 — 9,692 vs 9,592; port 8103 vs 8102) — and in a dense span (three or more comparable figures: a struct literal, coordinates) a typo-shaped pair decides only when the claim's skeleton aligns with the span's (a near-verbatim repetition with one figure swapped; "meta=335" against "meta=334, coding=241, …"), never across two different records (ball: x=365, y=560, r=8 against plunger = { x: 375, y: 560, … }). A span figure the claim itself also states ("the ball at x=365 … left of the wall at x=360" against channel = { x: 360 }) is never a misreading of the claim's other figure. Line-number prefixes (173:const …), dates, clocks and identifiers are masked before any of this. The model's relation label is never sufficient: "All 7 tasks finished" bound as contradict to "batch ran 5 task(s)" while the ledger listed seven (mined rec-a6e2b9b9b6) stays unchecked. And when the claim's own figure stands in another evidence line (figure_elsewhere: "35 °C" bound to "Temperature 34 °C" while the same tool printed "feels like 35 °C"), the binder bound the wrong reading — unchecked, whatever the relation and even when the pair is typo-shaped.
  4. Conflicting evidence. find_conflicting_line: for an agreeing claim, another evidence line with the same skeleton (packer [tool] labels stripped; figures and identifiers are the slots) and a different value at exactly the slot the claim reported is a conflict — the omitted-contradiction class (§4IA), mechanically, with the second line quoted in the issue. Two guards: when the reply states the twin's value too, it reports two records and omits nothing (reply_slots_of: two project ids, "from 1.42 to 1.55"); and in the audit a figure needs two digits to single out a field (a bare "1" agrees with EXIT CODE: 1 and would conflict with the next command's EXIT CODE: 0).
  5. Identifiers. IPv4 addresses, dotted versions of three or more parts, hex ids carrying letters and digits, UUIDs (_IDENT_RE) are tokens, never quantities (127.0.0.2 read as 127.0 hid a swapped last octet). audit_identifiers looks every one up verbatim — inline code included, that is where ids live — in evidence ∪ context: absent → unsupported (withholds CONFIRMED); present with a same-skeleton twin → conflicted (refutes).
  6. Implausible values. implausible_value: a labelled latitude outside [−90, 90] or longitude outside [−180, 360] in a claim contradicts it even when the tool printed it (run 8 of the IFS ask: "lat=128.11°").
  7. The number audit (audit_numbers, no model). The binder lists at most eight quotes; every prose figure of the reply (code fences, inline code, URLs and paths masked) is checked against every evidence figure with the same rules. Supported when one agrees — a figure only the context states (the user's own numbers restated) is supported by it; a supported figure whose evidence line has a same-skeleton twin differing only in that slot is conflicted (the omitted class caught without the binder having listed the claim: the mined Pinball rows, where the binder quoted ballX and the twin changed ballY); misreported — a validated contradiction — only when the pair is typo-shaped and near-miss and the reply sentence shares a subject word with the evidence line and the evidence figure is one the reply never states itself and the sentence is not dense (three or more figures: coordinates, specs); otherwise unsupported, counted and never a verdict (derived counts and conversions live there).
  8. The named-entity audit (audit_entities, no model). A fabricated sentence usually carries no figure ("The lead maintainer, Dr. Elin Vasquez, verified the result", "won the Meridian Prize", "confirmed by the Karlsen Institute" — the mined pool's 11/60 fabrications the binder confirmed because it never listed the appended sentence). Every Title-Case run of two or more words (connectors allowed) and every honorific-led name in the reply's prose — headings, table rows, block quotes, code and URLs excluded; a sentence-opening dictionary word ("Ask", "Per") is not part of the name — is looked up in the evidence and the context (the ask: a name the user used is not the reply's invention), as a phrase or as its tokens in any order. An unsupported entity withholds CONFIRMED (the evidence does not cover the reply) and never refutes (the reply may know it).
  9. The class checks (class_checks, §4IN, no model). Four failure classes quotes cannot see, decided by code and reusing the detectors the turn loop already trusts: artifact — machine noise in the reply's own text (objection._claim_noise_markers: SEARCH/REPLACE markers, hunk headers, ANSI, leaked <tool_call>; fenced and inline code excluded) → REFUTED, the marker quoted; constraint — a mechanically checkable constraint of the ask violated by the reply's shape (turn_state_check.refute_turn_state, §4FY: word / line / sentence caps, strict JSON, exact phrase, number-only) → REFUTED with the §4FY message; evidence — every evidence block is a tool failure or an empty retrieval (evidence_all_failed, delegating per block to the shared tool-error sniffer — status when the body carries one, prose rules otherwise — and to the turn's evidence gate; never a third failure vocabulary) → CONFIRMED withheld; topic — none of the ask's subject words (content words ≥4 letters, framing and stopwords removed) nor a shared five-letter stem appears in the reply (reply_off_topic) → CONFIRMED withheld. The two withholds never refute: a reply may honestly report the failure or paraphrase the topic.
  10. The residual judge with a quote burden (render_residual_prompt / apply_residual, §4IN; flag GHOST_CLAIM_BINDING_RESIDUAL = off | cheap | main). What code could not grade — a status word, a claim whose span the binder never found — goes to a model once, only when nothing has refuted, with the burden reversed: it may not say "unsupported"; per residual claim it must copy the evidence fragment that bears on it and name support | contradict | absent. Code accepts a relation only when the fragment is really in the evidence and shares a subject word with the claim (shares_subject: a content word, exactly or by a five-letter stem — "restarted"/"restart failed"). A validated contradict refutes with both quotes in the issue ("All tests passed" vs "3 tests failed, 12 passed"); a validated support agrees; an invented fragment, a fragment about something else, or absent leaves the row unchecked. Which model answers is a flag because the bench ranks them; see Measurement.
  11. Verdict (verdict_from_bindings). REFUTED iff at least one validated disagree, conflict, implausible, misreported or conflicted row — the issue names both quotes. CONFIRMED iff every surviving row agrees and no entity or identifier is unsupported (and, with GHOST_CLAIM_BINDING_STRICT_FIGURES=1, no figure either — measured on the mined pool: one clean confirm for one fact_swap, so off). UNCERTAIN otherwise: unbound rows are counted (with "evidence truncated" when the packer cut), unchecked rows are counted, an empty row list is UNCERTAIN at 0.4. Where the model says "contradict" but the figures agree (a hedged one-unit misreport), the row is unchecked — code and model disagreeing is not a verdict either way.

Consequences by construction: a subjective gloss ("a beautiful Saturday afternoon") is not a checkable claim and cannot be refuted; a validated disagreement refutes whatever the strong model would have said. The failure mode of everything else is UNCERTAIN. The parser (parse_binder_output) tolerates fences, string payloads and a row list cut by the token cap (complete {…} rows are salvaged).

Wiring

Verifier._verify_claim_binding renders the prompt, makes ONE cheap-leg call (json_only=True, critic_max_tokens=CLAIM_BINDING_MAX_TOKENS = 1024 — the binder's row list needs more than the 512 the critic cap gives a verdict; every other json_only caller keeps 512) and returns a VerifyResult whose issues are the validated pairs and whose escalation is "claim_binding". A validated REFUTED — both quotes in each issue — is returned as the verdict (escalation="claim_binding", binder_decided set, escalated_overturn copied unchanged from the incumbent — its own history — and the incumbent's cheap snapshot copied for the replay scorer); CONFIRMED and UNCERTAIN leave the incumbent's verdict untouched, so the classes the binder cannot check (constraints, artifact leaks, truncation, silent failure, wrong topic) keep their judge. The review round (§4IN R4–R6)

Three independent fresh-eye reviews — the consumers of the verdict, the module itself, and a replay of the model-free checks over 1,508 recorded turns — found 27 defects in this program's own code, 10 of them MAJOR. The live-dangerous ones: the context handed to verify_claim is project-constraint note + request, so the class checks had re-created the §4FD constraint-bleed (now ask_of reads only the request); _skeleton wildcarded clocks while _slots dropped them, so two rows of an hourly forecast or a log tail were "one field read twice" (clocks, dates, line numbers and identifiers are slots now); substring containment validated a truncated prefix (the count is 12 in …120 files); a month wildcard erased "3 separate" and "2 octets" as dates; a masked code fence made the number regex backtrack quadratically (26 s in a turn); the binder task leaked on caller cancellation. The corpus replay (491 turns the system had judged fine) went from 14 false refutes + 4 true to 0 false + 4 true (three STRICT-JSON violations, one 13-vs-14 line count), with the rules it forced: enumerations (three or more same-skeleton lines) and per-block meta lines never twin; a figure in an in-line list ("version 13 or 15") is never "the" field; a misread keeps its written shape (decimals equal); evidence-side URLs and citation lines carry no figures; a line over 600 characters anchors nothing; bounds ("over 160+", "up to 10 MB") agree on their side; the reply "states" an evidence figure at that figure's own precision and unit; heading ordinals, decades and thousands-group remnants are not figures. Integration: a binder-decided verdict writes an escalation-ledger row (outcome="claim_binding"), is binder_decided (escalated_overturn keeps recording whether the incumbent's own escalation bit — the incumbent's history, not the binder's), spells constraint issues as the turn loop's own tier does, and every direct caller of run_trials — the bench, the verifier-prompt optimizer, the objection replay — gets the live flags pinned off. Battery round 46: 45/45 killed.

The second round (§4IP R5–R6)

A second three-lens review of the post-review batch (the incumbent's uphold branch, confirm-first, the sidecar, the ledger report, the ddgs patch) and a replay of the cheap REFUTEDs in the bench response cache (first the 564 under the classic prompt; the third round extended it to the 2,233 under the two-stage adjudicate prompt, the live path) moved four rules here. Sentence ends: _SENT_END_RE treated any full stop after a digit as a decimal point, so "below 100,000. The sieve ran…" was one sentence and every sentence-level rule — dense, aligned, hedge — read two statements as one; only a stop between digits is a decimal point now. Relative time: "OTD 20 years ago" is a date anchored to the source's own unknown now, masked with the dates; a reply's "24-year span" had been protected from it only by the list number "2." in its sentence. The confirm-first switch lifts only (see the flag table). And claim_binding.py is now in the bench's provenance fingerprint beside objection.py, which delegates its shape rules here. Acceptance after the changes: bit-identical to the previous round on both cached pools (mined false-CONFIRM 0.062, seed 0.068, false-REFUTE 0/47), zero turns moved on the 1,512-turn corpus. Battery rounds 51/51b/51c/50b: 26 mutants plus a KNOWNBAD control, every one killed; the four NOOP controls survived. The third round (§4IP R7) — a code lens over the rules and an integration lens over the consumers — added the abbreviation mask to the sentence splitter ("approx. 29 users" keeps its hedge; "e.g.", "Dr.", "i.e."), moved the objection tier's name test onto the binder's own entity list and its claim-exclusivity test onto the quantity at the figure's position within its family, and closed the four consumer inconsistencies above (no lift over a strong-judge UNCERTAIN, the row after the appeal, no strong memo for a binder CONFIRMED, --outcome flips selecting on the shipped verdict). Battery 52: 16 mutants plus KNOWNBAD, every one killed — two only after their pins were hardened against the reload-contaminated enum identity (a pin comparing a collection-time VerifyVerdict member passes vacuously once a sibling file has reloaded the module).

The instruments lens of the third round audited the measurements rather than the rules: the grounded half of the objection tier's total-ungrounding rule had no pin (every fixture was short enough for the short-reply guard); the cache replay had covered only the classic prompt; three name guards were unpinned. It also surfaced, from the adjudicate population, a project name the reply took from its own project state (upheld as an invention — a name from the request or project note is now provenance, and a name that is the reply's stated next step is not a fact), rule 1 convicting a figure whose support sat in the packer's cut (the truncation floor now gates rule 1 too), and an HTTP status read as a counter-figure (the all-failed ground now decides before the numeric reading, and a figure on a tool-error line is the value of nothing). Battery 53: 16 mutants plus KNOWNBAD killed, one documented equivalent (the entity-shape fullmatch is masked by the binder's own entity list). The two gaps that round left were closed the same day: the active project's title now rides the verifier's request view (ACTIVE PROJECT: <title>, after the constraints clause and before || USER REQUEST:, through the same §4FD relevance gate as the constraints — so the binder counts it as support and the objection tier as provenance); and the entity shape learned the names the bench never measured — a run of particles ("Anneli van der Berg"), initials with a space after each ("J. K. Thornwood", not the tail of "U.S."), Greek and Cyrillic capitals with Greek honorifics ("Δρ. Ελένη Βασκέζ"). Greek names inflect and lose their tonos in capitals, so a non-Latin name token is compared accent-folded and, at six letters or more, on its stem ("Δημήτριο Κουφοντίνα" ← "Δημήτρης Κουφοντίνας"; Latin tokens keep whole-token matching); and because the cheap judge and English sources write Greek names in Latin letters ("Dr. Eleni Vaskez", "Kyriakos Mitsotakis"), a deterministic Greek→Latin fold (translit_greek) is tried on both sides — it only ever adds a match, for support here and for the objection tier's "written as a name" and "present" tests. A non-Latin sentence opener is always dropped (no word list can vouch for it). Single tokens ("Interpol") stay judge calls, and acronym bodies are not names (admitting them would make status labels and the agent's own node names into "absent names") — but a standards citation ("IEEE P2851", "ISO 8601", "RFC 3339", "CVE-2024-1234": a closed list of citation prefixes plus a code, capitals only) is an identifier: looked up verbatim, unsupported withholds a confirm, never a refute. That was the binder's one remaining false confirm on the class (6/27 mined acronym trials) — 0 after. The bench carries the shapes as an opt-in class fabrication_names (not in a default run, so cached pools stay comparable). Corpus replay after the change: 0 verdicts moved; 8 of 1,512 turns gained an entity withhold — every one a name the reply added that the digest did not carry.

§4IR — a validated withhold outranks an opinion

Refute-first and lift-only confirm-first left one exposure the §4IQ class made measurable: when the cheap judge confirms a reply that carries a fabricated name, the binder withholds (it never refutes on an absent name) but the incumbent's CONFIRMED still ships. The binder's withhold is code-validated — the name is in neither the evidence, nor the request/project note, nor an earlier turn's evidence — while the incumbent's confirm is an opinion. Since §4IR such a CONFIRMED ships capped at the withheld confidence: UNCERTAIN to every consumer, the label unchanged, no refute, no repair, no lesson scrub (claim_binding.name_withhold_caps_confirm, applied in _settle_claim_binding when the incumbent decides; the shadow row carries capped, the escalation ledger a withheld row, the report a CAPPED section). Guards: not on a digest cut past the truncation floor, never on a loopback address, and a name any earlier turn's evidence carried is exempt. Measured on the paired pools before shipping: incumbent false confirms removed 22/55 mined (every fabricated-name one, plus fact-swap and omission trials whose reply also names something the source did not), 10/57 seed, 15/15 and 6/6 on the §4IQ class; good confirms capped 3/49 mined, 0/34 seed, 3/49 and 0/28 on the class; on the 1,512-turn corpus 6 of 166 live good confirms (three of them the agent's own project title, which the request view now carries). A capitalised dictionary word right after a bold label or code span and a dash ("**Acquired Skills** — Two Python scripts") is no longer read as a name — two of the five bench costs were that.

§4IS-b — years are looked up, not rounded

The Αλκιβιάδου retry (probe-012ca9ec) delivered cleanly and gave the founders life spans — "(1850–1925)", "(1885–1975)" — that appeared in none of 26 tool outputs; nothing noticed, because the number audit drops bare years by design (a year is not a quantity to misreport by rounding) and the cheap judge looked elsewhere. audit_years now asks the other question: every bare year in the reply's prose (1800–2999, a whole token — not part of an id, a dimension, a version, a ratio or a path; not this year and its neighbours, which are the agent's own calendar) is looked up verbatim in evidence ∪ context, and an unsupported one withholds a confirm exactly as an unsupported identifier does — never a refute (a year from memory is often right), never the §4IR cap trigger, reported as "N year(s) not in the evidence" rather than as unfound figures. Two lessons from shipping it: a bare 1800s number stays a figure for the span comparison as well — widening the bare-year rule to 1800 made "total_orders 1847 (prev 1649)" lose its 1847 and refuted the reply's 1,847 against 1649 (the seed pool caught it at once; false-REFUTE 0/47 restored) — and a sentence-final stop is not a join. Cost measured before shipping: 7 of 475 good corpus turns carried a year the digest lacked (several of them dimensions the token rule excludes); after: 0 verdicts moved, no good confirm lost, seed false-CONFIRM 12 → 11.

§4IT — what the sources did not say, for the user

Everything above protects labels and lifts; none of it told the person reading the reply. Since §4IT the shipped verdict carries unverified_facts — computed once at the settle by claim_binding.unverified_facts from the binder's audits: bare years, standards citations and clearly named people or organisations (an honorific, or Latin Title-Case; a Greek capitalised phrase is too often a common noun in the genitive to be put in front of the user) that the reply states exactly and the whole session's sources carry nowhere — the test runs against the turn's raw tool outputs (all of them, uncut, up to 600 KB) plus the request/project note and earlier turns' evidence, not against the 12 KB digest: the first live probe showed the binder flag "1935, 1912 not in the evidence" while 1912 sat in a tool output the packer had left out, and the digest's truncation floor had rightly emptied the list; with the raw sources the floor does not apply and only the genuinely absent year is named. Never on a REFUTED; at most five. Delivery: on the non-streamed path _finalize_and_return ends the reply with one italic line — Not found in the sources I consulted: 1850, 1925, Dr. Elin Vasquez. — appended after the verdict was stamped so the judged-text fingerprint is the answer's; on the streamed path the late verdict queues the same list on the correction channel with its own kind, and the next reply of that conversation opens with ℹ️ On my previous answer: … (a caveat is not a correction — the answer stands). GHOST_VERIFY_SOURCE_CAVEAT=0 turns it off. Two packer changes shipped with it so the caveat does not flag what the digest merely failed to include: the claim pull's tokenizer reads Unicode words and bare years (a Greek reply produced no claim tokens under the old [a-zA-Z_] rule, so a 26-tool Greek research turn packed "6 items of 36" with no pull at all — and §4HO's sixth positional slot had filled the budget table, switching the pull off exactly on wide turns); the pull now adds up to two items chosen by marginal coverage of the claim's distinctive tokens, external sources only past the first. And a figure whose exact digits sit glued to letters in the evidence ("Πάρνηθος203", the pre-§4IO join) is "unsupported", never a misreport of some other line's figure — the corpus replay after the pull change had produced exactly one such false refute.
