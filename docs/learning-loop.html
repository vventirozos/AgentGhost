How it works

The learning loop

How a wrong answer becomes a rule Ghost follows and a test it has to keep passing — and why you, not Ghost, make the decision at each step that changes it.

The short version. Ghost does not teach itself. It notices when you were unhappy with an answer, works out — while you're away — what went wrong and what single rule would have fixed it, tries that rule on the same question, and then asks you. If you say yes, the rule goes into every answer from then on. Your complaint also becomes a regression test that is re-asked after every update, so a fix that later breaks is reported to you instead of going unnoticed.

Why it works this way

Until October 2026 Ghost ran many self-teaching passes: it reviewed its own failures, practised on invented exercises, extracted "skills", and wrote lessons for itself. Measured on real conversations, those lessons reached the turn they were meant for 18% of the time and changed the answer in 3 of 20 cases; some made answers worse. The judge it used to grade itself agreed with a wrong answer ("all systems green") two times out of two. Teaching itself, graded by itself, did not work.

The one signal that held up was yours. So the loop on this page is built around your judgement: Ghost does the slow work — replaying, diagnosing, testing — and you make the one decision that changes its behaviour. The self-teaching passes are switched off (listed below).

The whole loop

Orange outline = a step where you are involved. Nothing on the right-hand side changes Ghost's behaviour by itself.

Step by step

  1. Ghost records the turn

    Every answer to you is recorded with the tools it used and the pages it opened. Turns from other people (Slack members, public channels) and Ghost's own test runs are recorded too, but they never feed this loop — only your own turns can.

  2. Your reaction is the signal

    Two things count as you saying an answer was wrong: a 👎 on the reply (web page or Slack), and your next message showing it missed — "no, the latest is 18.6", "that's not what I asked". A small judge reads your next message while Ghost is idle and decides whether it was a correction. Other warning signs — the self-check doubting the answer, a run of failed tool calls — can put a turn on the list to be replayed, but they never become a test, and nothing is adopted on their word.

    core/owner_seeds.py — failure_signal, judge_reactions; core/feedback.py — the 👍/👎

  3. While you're away: replay it

    After about an hour of quiet, Ghost picks the newest such turn that only read things (searches, pages, files) — a request that changed something is never replayed — and asks itself the same question twice more. These replays are labelled as tests: they cannot write files, send messages or change memory, even if the model tries.

    core/failure_replay.py — pick_case, the base stage; one stage per idle slot

  4. Diagnose: what went wrong, and one rule

    The main model reads the original answer, the two replays and what each one actually saw, and writes down the cause and one general rule that would have prevented it — or says there isn't one. A rule that names the specific case, or that was obviously shaped by a web page's text, is thrown away.

    the diagnose stage

  5. Test the rule

    The question is asked twice more, this time with the candidate rule in the prompt. Then your kept regression tests are run with the rule too, so a rule that fixes one answer but breaks another shows up before you see it.

    the test and suite stages

  6. The proposal reaches you

    At the top of your next reply, under Background activity while you were away, you see one line that starts with the commands, then the rule, the evidence (what the self-check said before and after) and how your tests did. If you have push notifications set up (ntfy or a webhook), it arrives there too.

  7. You decide

    "learn rule N" adopts it. Ignoring it does nothing. Ghost never adopts a rule because its own judge liked it.

  8. The rule shapes every later answer

    An adopted rule goes into a short ### OWNER RULES block in the prompt of every one of your turns (up to six rules). It is not shown to Slack members or in public channels. "forget rule N" removes it after you confirm.

    core/agent.py — _owner_rules_block; memory/skills.py — owner_rules

The safety net: regression tests

A rule fixes the next answer; a test makes sure it stays fixed. The same 👎 that starts a replay also drafts a test proposal: your question, one sentence saying what a right answer must do, and up to four checks a program can run — for example "the reply contains 18.6", "it opened postgresql.org", "it did not say all systems green". No model grades a test; either the check passes or it doesn't.

  • You approve every test. A proposal does nothing until you say "keep test N", and it lapses after a week if you don't. At most three proposals wait at once.
  • Kept tests run by themselves. After every update to Ghost and once a day, while you're idle, two at a time. A failure — or a test that could not run — is reported to you the same way a proposal is.
  • They guard the rules. Every candidate rule is run against your kept tests before it is proposed, and the proposal says "your tests with it: 4/5 pass, FAILING: test 2".
  • They are test runs, not conversations. Read-only, labelled, never learned from.

What you can say

Each command has to be the whole message. The answer appears at the top of the reply, written by Ghost itself rather than the model.

A worked example, start to finish

This is the PostgreSQL case that shaped the loop. The wrong answer, the replays, the diagnosis, the tested rule and the final result are what actually happened in October 2026. That first round was run by hand, before the loop was automatic, and the rule was approved in a review session. The proposal, the "learn rule" command and the test proposal below show what the loop does with the same complaint today. In the real ledger this rule is rule 2.

  1. You ask, Ghost answers wrong

    You: "what is the latest version of postgresql?"
    Ghost: "The latest version is PostgreSQL 18.4." — twice, on two different days.

    The truth, on postgresql.org's versioning page: 18.6. Ghost had searched, read only the search snippets (an old LinkedIn post said 18.4), and never opened the vendor's page.

  2. You react

    A 👎 on the reply, or a next message like "that's out of date, it's 18.6". Either one marks the turn as your failure.

  3. Replay (while you're away)

    Ghost asks itself the question twice more. Both replays open the downloads page and the release notes this time — and answer "PostgreSQL 18, released 2025-09-25". Better sources, still wrong: they report the newest major version, not the newest release.

  4. Diagnose

    Cause: the latest major version was taken for the latest version, and the vendor's version table was never reached.
    Rule: "When asked for the latest version of software, give the latest point release, read from the vendor's own version, support or download table, and name the page."

  5. Test the rule

    Asked twice more with the rule: both answers now say 18.6, from postgresql.org/support/versioning. Checked on other software too, so the rule doesn't only fit this one case: Node.js and nginx were right without it and stayed right with it.

  6. The test proposal (what the suite adds today)

    From the same 👎, a test is drafted and shown to you:

    Test proposal 4 — say “keep test 4”, “edit test 4: …”, or ignore:
for “what is the latest version of postgresql?”, the answer names
the newest point release from postgresql.org
(checks: mentions one of 18.6; opens postgresql.org).

    You say "keep test 4". From then on it is re-asked after every update and daily. When PostgreSQL 18.7 ships the test will start failing — and you'll be told, which is your cue to edit test 4: must say 18.7. A test pins a fact; when the fact moves, you move the test.

  7. The proposal reaches you

    Background activity while you were away:
  - [self-play] Candidate rule 2 — say “show rule 2” to read it,
    “learn rule 2” to adopt it: When asked for the latest version …

  8. You decide

    You: "learn rule 2"
    Ghost (top of the reply, written by Ghost itself): "✓ Rule 2 adopted — saved as a lesson: “When asked for the latest version …”"

    From your next message on, the rule is in the ### OWNER RULES block of every prompt.

  9. The next time

    After the rule was adopted, the same question opened postgresql.org/support/versioning and answered 18.6, with the table of supported versions. The same question asked without the rule (a labelled control) answered "17". The rule is what made the difference.

Not every failure has a rule. In the same round, "how many people in the UK have been imprisoned for social media posts?" failed because the search engine returned junk and the good sources blocked the request. A rule ("say the sources were blocked") was tried and fixed it 0 times out of 2 — the problem was the tools, not the model's behaviour. A third case, a greeting answered with an invented "all systems green", got a rule that was adopted but that the model still doesn't follow on small talk; that one was left as is. The loop shows you this evidence instead of pretending every failure is fixable by a sentence in the prompt.

How to see it working

In the chat
"show all rules" and "show regression tests". Proposals and test failures appear under Background activity while you were away at the top of your next reply. Routine outcomes — an abandoned replay, a test run where everything passed — go only to the activity ledger, so they don't clutter the chat.
Ask Ghost
"what did you do while I was away?" — it reads the activity ledger (introspect, action activity), not its own recollection.
On disk
$GHOST_HOME/system/selfplay/failure_replays.json — every replay case, its stages, replies, diagnosis and verdicts.
$GHOST_HOME/system/regression/cases.json — every test, its checks, its state and its run history.
The adopted rules are playbook rows with origin owner_rule.
In the log
Rule Command and Test Command lines for your commands; the idle slot's outcome line for each replay stage and test run.

What is switched off, and why

These used to run on their own. Each was measured on real turns, found not to help, and switched off in October 2026. The code is still there behind a switch, so any of them can be turned back on with an environment variable set to 1 — but nothing on this page depends on them.

WhatWhat it didWhy it is off
Self-written lessons
GHOST_PRODUCER_LESSONS		Reflection, post-mortems and memory consolidation wrote lessons for itself.Reached their turn 18% of the time; changed the answer in 3 of 20. Only your rules are written now.

research: removed; coding: GHOST_CODING_PRACTICE		Solved made-up problems to learn from them.Research practice passed 8 of 9 at the first try — nothing to learn — and was replaced by replaying your real failures. Coding practice was switched off with it, at your request.
Automatic skills
GHOST_AUTO_SKILLS		Turned repeated tool sequences into "skills".Trivial sequences that quoted old requests back into 19 of 86 prompts.

GHOST_BUS_GRAPH_TIER, GHOST_BUS_EPISODIC_TIER		Added related memories to every prompt.20% / 15% relevant, 16% / 19% harmful. Facts about you stay on; recall on request stays on.

GHOST_ROUTER, GHOST_FORESIGHT, GHOST_RISK_GOVERNOR, GHOST_COMPETENCE_PROMPT		Per-turn predictions and steers.Nothing used their output, or it was measurably wrong.

The self-check on every answer still runs, and its confidence is still used — but as a ranking: the least confident 15% of answers get the deeper check. It is not treated as a probability, because measured against outcomes it isn't one. Details: core.agent §4ND.

Limits, honestly

  • It needs you. With no 👎 and no corrections, the loop does nothing. That is intended: there is no signal it can trust without you.
  • Only read-only questions. A request that wrote a file or sent a message is never replayed, so failures of that kind get no rule and no test.
  • Slow by design. One stage per idle slot: a replay case takes several idle periods to reach you. On a busy day nothing moves.
  • A rule is a sentence in the prompt. The model can ignore it, as with the greeting rule. A kept test is how you find out.
  • Tests pin facts that move. A "latest version" test will fail when the version changes. That failure is correct — edit the test.
