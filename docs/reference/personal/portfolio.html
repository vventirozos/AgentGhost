tools / portfolio.py

The portfolio tool: the owner's IBKR holdings, read from a separate service. 2026-11-06

Why it is a separate service

The agent stays on Tor and holds no keys for outside accounts. A brokerage connection is tied to the owner by nature, so it lives in interface/ibkr-service, on another machine, on the normal internet, by the operator's choice. The agent only reads text from it over the tailnet. See the service's README.md for setup and operation.

Configuration and visibility

  • GHOST_PORTFOLIO_URL: the service's address, such as http://100.64.0.2:8095. Unset means the tool isn't advertised.
  • GHOST_PORTFOLIO_KEY_FILE, default ~/Data/AI/.ibkr_service_key, mode 0600. An empty file also hides the tool. The key is redacted from stored text by value.
  • Members never get it: it's outside _MEMBER_ALLOWED_TOOLS, and the tool refuses a member by itself as well. Contained contexts have it in their forbidden set.

The buy advisor (advise)

The owner gives a sum, for example 500 in the account's currency, and optionally the opportunity cash already held aside. The service splits it in two. The rules and settings are in the service's README.md; nothing happens until the owner sets advisor_core in the service's config.json.

  1. Core (90% by default): each core fund gets a cheapness score from 0 to 1 against its own five years (fall from its 52-week high, gap to its 200-day average, last month's return, each ranked against every past day). The fund's aim is multiplied up to 3 times when cheap and down to a third when dear, and pulled back when the holdings have drifted. A fund well above its aim gets nothing that time. The fund marked safe takes everything when it is clearly cheap and the cheapest. The result is in whole shares.
  2. Opportunity (10%, plus the cash held aside, capped at three slices; the rest joins the core): a watchlist of tech-leaning UCITS funds in EUR and the core funds are checked for a fall of at least twice the fund's usual move over a day, a week or a month, while 10% or more below the 52-week high. Single stocks are not candidates. The most unusual fall the money can buy in whole shares gets it; with none, the reply says to keep it as cash for the next one.
  3. The agent reports the service's amounts unchanged. For a hot name it searches the news for why it fell, because the service sees prices only. It reads at least one page from an established outlet before naming a cause and names that source; a claim seen only in search snippets or on one fringe site is reported as an unconfirmed rumour.

Cheapness describes where a price sits against its own past, not where it goes next. The reply says so, names every missing price or history, says when drift from the aims is unknown, flags a core fund that matches no holding (holdings kept from an earlier plan, advisor_kept, are named as kept instead), compares the buys with the statement's cash, and mentions order costs. A price that jumps more than 25% from the last close (a split, another listing) is left out rather than read as a crash.

Measured, not assumed. A month-by-month replay on 2022–2026 prices showed the first design's single-stock slice ending well behind plain 60/30, which is why the slice is funds-only and capped. The final plan ended within 0.3% of plain 60/30: it keeps the buying disciplined, and it has no measured edge.

Results

Every figure is computed by the service, and the tool passes its text through unchanged. A failure starts with Error:, so the turn loop sees it through result_is_failure. Old or missing data is labelled inside a successful result, as STALE or Data gap, because the figures that remain are still usable.

What it can't do

It can't trade. The service has no order code and accepts only GET, and Flex only hands out reports. The agent never sees the Flex token or the account id, which the service never stores.
