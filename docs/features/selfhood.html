Mood and character

The agent has a mood that moves with what happens, wears off by itself, and is audible in how it words its replies, never in what they say. It also has a character that evolves: three dials (warmth, humour, directness) that move by themselves, a step at a time, from how its days go, and one sentence that says them and shapes how its replies to you are phrased.

Runs on: Mac Studio (eva) + Mac mini (nova)

What it does

The character

The agent has a character: three dials, each a whole number from −2 to +2, and one sentence that says them. The sentence is put in front of the model on every reply it writes to you in private, as "how you see yourself".

Dial−2+2Starts at
warmthdry and unsentimentalopenly warm and fond−1, a little dry
humourearnest, with no jokesplayful, joking wherever there is room+1, fond of a light joke
directnesstactful to a faultblunt, saying it as it is0, neither

So it starts as "I am a little dry and fond of a light joke." All three dials are about voice. None is about length or effort, on purpose: in a blind measurement a character that was "short and dry" also made a third fewer tool calls. The rule under the sentence says it shapes how things are phrased and never how much is done, and that the traits are never named out loud.

The character moves slowly, one step at a time:

  • By your word, as an override. Type character to see it, character humour up or character warmth -2 to set a dial, character pin warmth to hold one, character veto to take back the last move it made by itself. The system answers these; no model is involved.
  • By itself, from how its days go (§4UH). Each closed day is read for three signals, one per dial, all things the agent already counts: warmth from how you talk to it (your messages that sound grateful, admiring, approving or amused against those that sound annoyed or disapproving, read by an emotion model; thanks and message tone on days before it), humour from how its own work went (clean turns, relief and pride against trouble), directness from how its answers were judged (thumbs up against thumbs down and corrections). No signal feeds two dials: since 2026-11-12 your thumbs move directness only, so a good day of thumbs no longer moves warmth with it. A day pulls a dial only when it stands out from the agent's own last three weeks, so an owner who always thumbs up does not push warmth to the top. When, over two weeks since the dial last moved, the days pulling one way lead the other way by three (four to move further from 0), the dial moves one step. Replayed over the 45 real days before it was switched on, this moved the dials nine times and ended at warmth 0, humour +1, directness +2.
  • It no longer rates its own replies. From 2026-11-11 to 2026-11-14 the agent read six of its recent replies once a day and rated them on the same three scales (the "self-look"); its vote was already off. It was removed on 2026-11-14 (§4UH-noself): a calibration rated 40 samples of replies written with no character, with three different dial settings and with a strong "short and dry, blunt" sentence, and every pool came back about +1, +1, +1 — where blind readers had told those characters apart 75–97 %. The model rating itself cannot see the character, so the look said nothing. Only the day records move a dial.

How it has changed so far. It started on 2026-11-11 as warmth −1, humour +1, directness 0: "I am a little dry and fond of a light joke." At the close of its first day on its own (2026-11-12, 04:38) warmth moved to 0 and directness to +1, both from day signals, with no command and no vote: "I am fond of a light joke and straight about what I think." In a blind test after that move, readers told replies with the new sentence from replies without it in 75–80 % of pairs, and no reply named a trait. Type character to see where it is now and how it got there.

Your commands stay: they are an override and a correction, never what makes the character move. Every change is logged with what was counted, so "how have you changed?" has a real answer.

Tastes

The agent has come to like some kinds of work. Every one of your turns is filed under the kinds its tools say it was (research, coding, databases, pictures, projects, memory, looking after the system, the portfolio), as having gone well or badly: half its calls failed, it was never delivered, or you gave it a thumb. What is counted fades with a half-life of 30 days. A kind is liked when it goes badly half as often as the agent's average or less, and rough when twice as often or more, over enough turns to mean something. Your replies hear the liked kinds only, as one line under the character, with the rule that a liking is a feeling about the work and never a reason to choose or avoid a tool. What has been rough never reaches the model, not through introspect and not through the character reply either (a reply goes back to the model as history): a dislike could change what it does. It is written in the agent's log when days are counted.

The character and the mood

The dials also change how hard events land on the mood, by a quarter per step and at most a half either way (since 2026-11-12; a tenth before, which a replay of 30 days showed was too faint to notice). A warm character feels your thumbs, thanks, corrections and the tone of your messages more, a dry one less. A playful character takes its own bad turns lighter, a serious one heavier; its good turns are untouched. Directness changes how it talks, not what it feels. The scaling is done when an event arrives, before it is booked, so the mood's journal, its reaction ledger and its caps all hold what really moved. GHOST_CHARACTER_LEAN=0 switches this part off.

The day record

Once a day is over (it ends at 04:00), the agent writes one row about it: how long each mood was held, the biggest events, what its own work did (trouble, clean turns, tasks finished), and what you did (thumbs, corrections, thanks, moods and manners you set). It is counted from the mood's own journals with no model call, and it holds no message, task name or subject. The character's daily rule reads these rows. At the deploy of §4UH the rows were rebuilt for the 45 days before it from the journals and written in once, marked seeded, so the dials had a history to compare against, and evidence to move on, from the first day.

A shade, for a moment

A mood has one of nine labels, and "content" reads the same whatever made it. A shade keeps what the mood is about, for one moment: relieved (a turn went well soon after one of its own went badly), proud (a project task was finished), chastened (you corrected the last reply or gave it a thumbs-down). The next reply to you carries one light line of it and no reply after that does; the face shows the same moment. It never moves the mood's numbers.

The strange loop

The writer Douglas Hofstadter argued that an "I" is a strange loop: a system that builds a picture of itself, and whose behaviour is then shaped by that picture, which is in turn redrawn from the behaviour. The character is the agent's small version of this, and every arrow in it is real:

  1. The dials make one sentence, and every reply to you in private hears it as "how you see yourself".
  2. Those replies bring something back: your thumbs, thanks, corrections and tone, how its own work went, how its answers were judged.
  3. Each day is closed into a day record, and the daily rule compares it with the agent's own last three weeks.
  4. When the days add up, a dial moves, so the sentence changes, and the next replies hear the new one.

Type mirror to see where the loop has gone: each sentence the character has had, what moved it (by itself, with the counts, or by you), and where it is now. The system shows it; no model is involved.

Before the character there was the Mirror (2026-11-02 to 2026-11-11): once a day the model rewrote a one-sentence self-portrait from its own replies. Measured blind, that sentence changed nothing in them: it described the model's own default voice. A sentence that differs from that voice, in the same place, was picked out by readers in 93 to 97 % of pairs, which is why the character replaced it. The Mirror's code and its diary were removed on 2026-11-12.

The mood

The mood is two numbers: pleasantness and energy. Both halve every two hours, so a mood wears off by itself. From the pair the agent reads one of nine labels and a level:

Low energyEnergy near 0High energy
Pleasantcalmcontentcheerful (or curious after real research)
Near 0boredneutral (no mood)restless
Unpleasanttireddownirritable

Level 1 is a faint tint in word choice. Level 2 is clearly noticeable, with a remark or two in character. At level 3 the agent may say outright that it is in a mood. The automatic mood has no cap: it can reach 3 on its own.

The character's resting point

The character's dials set where the mood rests when nothing is happening (since 2026-11-12): pleasantness from warmth and humour (their average, never below even), energy following humour and never larger than the pleasantness. The dials are traits, not moods: dryness, seriousness or bluntness never makes the rest gloomy, restless or bored. A warm or playful one rests a little sunny; playful adds a little brisk, serious a little calm; directness sets no rest. This rest is small and only shows in the numbers and in the face's tint. It never tilts which mood is heard or how strong it is. In the other direction, only what lasts reaches the character: a reply written under a strong mood or a manner is never shown to the daily look, and a mood that came back on several of the last seven days reaches it as one note.

How it works

What moves the mood

  • The agent's own turns. Failed tool calls and guards stepping in make it irritable, weighted by the share of calls that failed. A clean multi-tool turn lifts it. A research turn that read three or more pages makes it curious. A very long turn tires it (or just calms it, if it went well). Closing a project task lifts it. Transient failures such as a Tor timeout do not count.
  • Your reactions. Thumbs up or down, a correction of the last reply, and short thanks or praise ("thanks", "well done", "μπράβο", a lone 👍). Each reply has one ledger, so a thumbs-down, the correction that came with it and an unhappy tone are not counted three times.
  • The tone of your messages. A sentiment model rates each English message you send. What your upbeat messages added and what your unhappy ones took off are kept as two separate numbers, each capped at 0.12, below the 0.20 a mood needs to be heard, so your tone alone never makes a heard mood; it can only add to one something else made. A tone counts for what it changed: an upbeat message about a reply you had already thumbed up adds nothing more. Doubtful scores (upbeat words about trouble, which may be sarcasm, or a task worded harshly) are held back. Code, quotes and logs are removed before scoring.
  • Your word. Setting a mood or manner in chat. Only you, in a message of your own, can do it; a scheduled task, a background job or a test is refused. In a Slack thread where someone else has written, your own message must say "mood" or "manner", or name the one you want ("be cheerful", "manner evil", "mood: neutral", "cheer up"), and each thing set is checked on its own: "be calm" sets a mood, never a manner too. A quoted line (starting with >) is not yours. Another member's line there never sets it.

Only your real turns move it. A plain chat turn with no tools moves nothing. A channel member's turn, a scheduled report or a test never moves it.

Boredom

After six quiet hours a neutral mood reads as bored (level 2 after twelve). It is never stored and never heard in a reply: whoever writes next is not answered bored. When you come back, the quiet simply ends. The quiet is counted from your last turn or from the last time a background job you started did work, whichever is later, so the agent does not read bored while it works for you.

Audible, not changed

When there is a mood or manner, one line is added to the agent's per-turn notes: the mood, its level, and the rule that it changes how the reply is phrased and nothing else. Same facts, numbers, tool use and completeness; no refusals; code and commands carry no tone. The order is fixed: your rules, then correctness, then the manner, then the mood, then the character. Everyone the agent answers hears the tone, but channel members are never told that you set it, and never get your private sentiment share: what they hear is never stronger than it would be had no message tone been read at all.

The face

In the web console and on the handheld, the agent's face is an animated mood tesseract: a glowing network around a red kernel, with a temperament layer that reacts to tool calls, thinking and quiet without ever repeating itself exactly. The mood tints it: low energy cooler, high energy warmer, stronger at higher levels. While the mood is neutral it shows the resting point faintly.

The mood is also written out in words, because a look cannot be read exactly. On the handheld they are the first item of the status line; in the web console they are in the status chip's tooltip (the words under the message box were removed on 2026-11-12 at the owner's request). They are one short phrase: calm, 1 of 3; rests calm when the mood is neutral and the face shows the resting point; neutral; and, when a manner is set, its level and end, as in manner evil, 2 of 3 — until 04:00. The words and the look are made from the same label, which the agent sends as one field, so they cannot disagree. The words stay when animations are off. The web console's status chip adds the cause and, for a mood you set, until when it holds.

The clients read the mood every 25 seconds, and once more about a second and a half after a reply ends or a thumb is recorded, so a mood you just set shows without waiting for the next reading. When three readings in a row bring no usable mood (the agent is down, or sends something the client cannot read), the face eases back to neutral and the words say mood unknown. When the agent could not read its own state file at start, the words say so: mood unreadable when there is no mood to show, or added after the mood (cheerful, 2 of 3 · state file was unreadable at start) when there is one.

A thumb can be taken back: click the lit thumb again in the web console, or press the lit chip again on the handheld. The mood moves back by what that thumb still held. The turn keeps its label.

Since §4UG the face shows three more things. The mood moves continuously: the look is mixed from the two numbers behind the label, so you can watch a mood build and fade where it used to step between three strengths. Moments: when the mood gets a shade, the face plays one gesture a few seconds long — a release and lift for relief, a warm front running outward for pride, a droop and dim when chastened — once, and never again for the same shade. The character: the dials set how the face carries itself: humour how often it fidgets and with which gestures, warmth its light and tint, directness how hard events land on it. With reduced motion the moments show in light only. To try them by hand in the web console: __ghostFace.playMoment('relieved'), __ghostFace.previewMoodAt(-0.5, 0.6), __ghostFace.previewCharacter({humour: 2}); each called with no argument goes back.

Looking inside: introspect

The introspect tool reads back the mood and its cause, the day's timeline, the diary, recent experiences, background activity and stats. It is the honest answer to "how are you?", read from stored records rather than invented. Where other people read the reply (a public channel) it shows the mood, the manner and their levels only: no cause, no timeline, no diary, no work log, no background activity, and not who set a mood or manner or until when it holds.

Where it runs

  • The mood, the journal, the character, the day record and introspect live in the agent on the Mac Studio (eva). None of them calls a model.
  • The sentiment service runs on the Mac mini (nova): a RoBERTa sentiment model behind a fastText language check that lets only English through.
  • The face runs in your browser and on the handheld; it reads the mood from the agent's health endpoint.

Limits and switches

  • The character is heard only in replies to you on a private surface, members and public channels hear the mood only. GHOST_CHARACTER=0 switches it off: no sentence is heard, the mood rests at even, and the dials do not move; the day record and the shade stay.
  • A dial moves at most one step at a time and needs at least three days that stand out; a week of ordinary days moves nothing. Tastes need at least eight turns of a kind. A day's thumbs are read from your labels: a thumb you take back still counts for that day, and a thumb changed on a later day counts on both days.
  • How a character sounds depends on the model. scripts/character_probe.py runs the same message with and without it as labelled test requests and reports whether a blind reader can tell, whether tool use dropped, and whether any reply named a trait.
  • The mood is a style, not a feeling claim, and it never changes an answer. The character is three dials and one sentence about voice, not a model of consciousness.
  • The sentiment model reads English only. Greek praise and corrections are recognised by word lists, but Greek tone is not scored.
  • The self-image is shown only in replies to you on a private surface, never to members or public channels. A labelled test request can ask for a reply without it (X-Ghost-Arm: no_self_image beside X-Ghost-Origin: probe); that is how its effect on replies is measured, and none of your own messages can lose it this way.
  • A day, for the mood, ends at 04:00: your set mood, a manner, "today" in the timeline, the day record, and the character's daily rule all use that day.
  • A stored time that lies more than a day ahead of the clock (28 hours for the end of a hold) is treated as expired, so a wrong clock cannot hold a mood for ever. The first read that finds one removes it from the file: a hold that expired this way does not come back.
  • If the mood's state file cannot be read at start, the mood starts neutral, the file is kept, renamed state.json.bad, and the agent says so when asked about its mood, until a mood is next written.
  • The same thumb sent again (a client's retry around a restart) counts once; a thumb you took back and give again counts again, unless the agent restarted in between.
  • A thumb can be taken back until the agent restarts (the reaction ledger is in memory) and as long as you have not set the mood since. It withdraws the thumb from the mood only.
  • Undo goes back one step, does not bring a message tone back, and does not undo "neutral".
  • The check on a thread with someone else's message is advisory: a mood's name is an everyday word ("keep calm and summarise"), and the model still decides whether to call the tool. It reads English words.
  • The voice (text-to-speech) has no tone control, so the mood is in the words only.
  • A manner has no look of its own: the face has no spare signal for it, so a manner shows in the words only.
  • The extra reading after a reply is one reading at a fixed delay. If the agent has not moved the mood by then, the change shows at the next 25-second reading.
  • Mood unknown appears after three failed readings, about 75 seconds; until then the last look and words stay.
  • The words under the message box are cut with an ellipsis on a narrow phone when a mood and a manner are both set; the full text is in the tooltip, which a phone cannot open.
  • Switches: GHOST_SENTIMENT_URL (unset = no sentiment), GHOST_MIRROR_REST=0 turns the resting point off. If the sentiment service is down, the mood simply goes without it.
