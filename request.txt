Conduct an open-source and dark-web intelligence investigation into the September 2026 Revolut customer-data incident described by Reuters as involving fraudulent government requests. Objective: Identify, if possible, the actual government agency, country, email domain, or email address used to send the fraudulent requests to Revolut. Known facts to anchor the investigation Reuters reported on 12 September 2026 that Revolut disclosed a sensitive customer-data breach caused by fraudulent government requests. Revolut reportedly said an unauthorized email account was created inside a legitimate government authority's domain infrastructure. The request reportedly passed normal email-authentication mechanisms such as SPF/DKIM/DMARC. Data reportedly disclosed included customer identity/KYC information and Bitcoin transaction history. Public reporting has attributed a customer notification/screenshot to ZachXBT. Revolut has not publicly identified the government agency or country. Online speculation has mentioned the US, France, Romania, Italy, Spain and the UK, but none is currently substantiated. Search targets Search broadly across: Reddit and Reddit mirrors/archives. X/Twitter posts and mirrors. Telegram channels and indexed Telegram archives. Cybersecurity researchers and threat-intelligence blogs. BreachForums, Exploit, RaidForums mirrors/archives, and other indexed cybercrime forums. Paste sites and public leak indexes. Search-engine caches and archived webpages. Crypto-investigator posts, especially material associated with ZachXBT. Screenshots/images of the original Revolut customer notification. Any references to the actual sender address, government domain, request number, agency name, or email headers. Search strategy Do NOT search only for "Revolut breach." Use combinations such as: Revolut + government request + email Revolut + government domain + data leak Revolut + unauthorized email account Revolut + SPF DKIM DMARC Revolut + government agency + Bitcoin Revolut + legal request + customer data Revolut + ZachXBT Revolut + "created directly within" Revolut + "government authority's domain" Revolut + "urgent security update" Revolut + passport + Bitcoin transaction history Revolut + KYC + government email Exact fragments from the customer notification in quotation marks Any discovered email/domain fragments combined with Revolut Also search for images/screenshots rather than text only. Look for reposts, crops, OCR text, thumbnails, filenames, image hashes, and quoted descriptions of screenshots. Critical forensic requirements For every alleged domain or email address: Provide the exact string. Give the original source URL/location and date. Preserve the surrounding context. Determine whether the source is firsthand, secondhand, or speculation. Check whether the domain actually belongs/belonged to a government organization. Check historical DNS, MX, SPF, DKIM and DMARC records where legally/publicly available. Check certificate-transparency records and historical WHOIS/RDAP where available. Determine whether the domain's mail infrastructure could legitimately send authenticated mail. Distinguish between: - spoofed domain, - look-alike/typosquat, - compromised employee account, - attacker-created mailbox inside a legitimate tenant/domain, - compromised mail server, - compromised third-party email provider. If an IP address or mail header is found, correlate it only with publicly available infrastructure/intelligence data. Attribution standard Do NOT infer the country simply from the nationality of affected Revolut customers. Do NOT treat anonymous forum claims as evidence merely because multiple accounts repeat them. Rank findings: Confirmed: directly supported by an original document, email/header, government statement, Revolut statement, or independently verifiable technical evidence. Strongly supported: multiple independent sources plus technical corroboration. Plausible: technically/geographically consistent but lacking confirmation. Rumour: single-source or anonymous claim. False/misleading: contradicted by evidence. Important safety boundary Do not log into, compromise, exploit, purchase, download stolen personal data, or interact with criminal services. Do not retrieve or reproduce victims' passwords, authentication tokens, financial credentials, passports, addresses, or other unnecessary personal information. If a forum contains the suspected email address, report only the minimum information necessary to establish the domain/agency identity and redact unrelated personal data. Final deliverable Produce a forensic report with: Best-supported government country Government agency Email domain Exact sender address, if publicly exposed Evidence chain Original source(s) Technical validation of the domain Timeline Competing country/agency theories Confidence score (0–100%) What remains unknown A list of every materially relevant source, including dark-web source identifiers where safe to disclose Most importantly: if the evidence does not establish the country/domain, say so. Do not manufacture an attribution.
